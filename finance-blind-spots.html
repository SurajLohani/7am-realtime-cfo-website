Finance Blind Spots: The Problems Your Reports Don't Show You

A blind spot is a finance problem that exists while every report still looks fine. Profit shows, but cash is missing. Sales grow, but contribution quietly shrinks. The numbers are all there, but nobody can say what decision they point to. Finding these early is what a Fractional CFO is for.

Run the Free Financial Blind Spot Diagnostic
Six Kinds of Blind Spot

Where the money, control and time quietly leak

Each one hides behind reports that look correct. Each one is found the same way: follow the number back to its source, then to the decision it should have driven.

Cash

Cash blind spots

Cash trapped despite reported profit.

  • Profit on the P&L, but no clear answer on where the cash went
  • No forward view of the next 13 weeks
Profit & Margin

Profit and margin blind spots

Revenue growing while contribution quietly deteriorates.

  • Margin eroding through a contract nobody re-read
  • Costing and pricing that no longer match reality
Working Capital

Working capital blind spots

Inventory, receivables and payables consuming cash.

  • Ageing stock nobody is tracking
  • Receivables and payables out of step with the cash cycle
Control

Control blind spots

Reconciliation gaps, leakage, duplicate payments and unusual variances.

  • Differences between books, bank and records that stay unexplained
  • Leakage hidden in clean-looking reports
Forecasting

Forecasting blind spots

Management seeing yesterday instead of what is coming next.

  • Budgets set once and never revisited
  • No scenario view when costs, demand or funding change
Decision

Decision blind spots

Numbers available, but no clear decision attached to them.

  • Reports full of data, with no recommendation
  • Leadership meetings that review numbers but do not decide
How a Fractional CFO Finds Them

From cash to decision, one connected chain

Finance first, then business, then decision, then technology. The chain below is the order in which the questions are asked.

Cash→Profit→Margin→Inventory→Working Capital→Controls→Forecasting→Decision
1

Understand the problem

Start with the business question, not the report: where does the cash, margin or control worry actually sit?

2

Verify the data

Reconcile books to bank and records before trusting any number. Data first.

3

Trace to the cause

FP&A and forensic thinking follow each gap back to its source and its owner.

4

Fix the system and decide

Controls, MIS and automation are added only where they close the gap, and every finding ends in a decision a human owns.
