Budget vs Actual: How to Read a Variance Without Blaming the Wrong Person

Suraj Kumar Lohani · 2 min read
FP&A Budgeting Variance

A budget variance is a question, not a verdict. When actual cost is above budget, the first useful move is to ask why, not who.

Split the variance into three causes

  • Volume. You sold or produced more or less than planned, so everything linked to volume moved.
  • Price or rate. The unit cost or unit price differed from the plan.
  • Timing. The spend or income landed in a different month from where it was budgeted, and will correct itself.

Only after separating these can you tell whether a variance is a problem. A cost that is higher because volume was higher may be good news.

Compare like with like

If volumes changed, restate the budget at actual volume (a flexed budget) and compare actual cost against that. Otherwise a higher-sales month will look over budget on every variable cost line.

Focus on the few lines that matter

Set a materiality threshold, for example any line more than a chosen percentage and amount away from budget. Explain those lines in one sentence each. A report that explains everything explains nothing.

A good variance comment names the driver, the amount and the action. 'Freight up due to a rate change, +2 lakh, renegotiating next quarter' beats 'freight high'.

Keep the conversation fair

Share the variance with the person who controls the line, before the meeting. Ask what they see. Most surprises have an operational reason that the numbers alone will not show.
